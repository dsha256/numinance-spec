<ShortProductTruth>
The breaker lives in your runtime and depends on nothing outside it. If the vendor, the control plane or the network disappears, you can still stop the agent.
</ShortProductTruth>

## Four positions

Positions are ordered from least to most restrictive. Consumers compare by that order, never by number alone.

| Position | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `FULL_AUTO` | Caps nothing beyond the autonomy policy |
| `PR_ONLY` | Caps effective autonomy at opening pull requests |
| `READ_ONLY` | Allows no write |
| `STOPPED` | Pauses every run |

`FULL_AUTO` does not mean unrestricted. It means the breaker adds no cap, and floors, policy, grants and budgets all still apply.

## Relation to the autonomy ladder

The ladder is policy: Observe, Suggest, Implement, PR, Merge, Stage, Release, Operate. The breaker is a separate override above it. `PR_ONLY` is a **breaker state**, not a ladder level. When the breaker is at `PR_ONLY`, the effective level for any work is the lower of the policy level and PR. When it is at `READ_ONLY`, no writing action is allowed whatever policy says.

```text
effective autonomy = min(policy level, breaker cap)
```

This means you can incident-respond without editing policy, and you can restore normal operation by resetting the breaker rather than rewriting configuration.

## What trips it

Designed triggers include:

- A human, through the dashboard, CLI or API.
- The incident detector on an abnormal action.
- Budget or policy anomalies.
- A failure to write evidence.
- Provider drift, such as an extension whose declared permissions no longer match.
- A sandbox canary violation at runtime.

## What happens when it trips

1. Credential handles are revoked.
2. Runs pause at the next checkpoint. A change shows as paused rather than failed.
3. Effective autonomy is capped at the new position.
4. The trip, its source and its reason are recorded as evidence.

Reset needs a human and a reason, and the reset is recorded too. An agent identity cannot reset the breaker.

## Customer-local by construction

Breaker state is held in the customer data plane and is evaluated by the runtime on every privileged action. It does not call the control plane, so:

- A control plane outage cannot leave the breaker unreachable or stale.
- A control plane compromise cannot reset it.
- Sovereign and later air-gapped modes behave the same way.

The dashboard may show and change the breaker for convenience, but it is a client of the local state, not its owner.

## Break-glass

Break-glass is the exception path for a human who must act outside normal gates, for example to unblock an urgent fix while approvers are unavailable.

- **Human only.** Named human principal with strong authentication. No agent identity can invoke it.
- **Named.** The actor is individual and attributable, not a shared account.
- **Time-boxed.** It expires. The design sets a short default expiry.
- **Scoped.** It covers a stated scope, not general override.
- **Logged separately.** Events go to a distinct log and into evidence.
- **Reviewed.** A post-event review task is created automatically.

Dual control can be required for specific policy packs.

## Example operation

<IllustrativeNote>
Descriptive flow, not a command transcript. The breaker commands are not in the open <code>numi</code> build.
</IllustrativeNote>

A verifier starts reporting false accepts. The on-call operator moves the breaker to `PR_ONLY`. In-flight work that would merge now stops at pull request, and nothing is lost. After investigating, the operator moves it to `FULL_AUTO` with a recorded reason, and work that stopped at PR resumes through normal approval.

## Failure modes

- **Breaker set but never reviewed.** A long-lived `PR_ONLY` hides a policy that needs fixing. Review trips as incidents.
- **Cap on the wrong scope.** Confirm the breaker is global to the runtime and not mistaken for a per-playbook setting. Per-playbook limits belong in policy.
- **Evidence-write failure.** This both trips the breaker and means recent records may be missing, so verify the chain after recovery.
