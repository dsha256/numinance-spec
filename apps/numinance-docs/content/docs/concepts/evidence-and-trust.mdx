## What evidence is

For every change the runtime writes an append-only chain of records under the runner identity: decisions, actions, denials, verifier results and outcomes. The chain is exported as a portable `evidence/v1` directory that the open verifier checks offline, without the engine and without network access. Decisions that were denied are in the chain, which is why a clean-looking history is more than a list of successes.

## Trust roots belong to the verifier

A chain whose hashes agree proves only that the records agree with each other, and anyone can build one. Trust comes from keys and a log origin the **caller** chooses in advance. There is no built-in root, and a key supplied by the export itself never earns the top result.

## Three levels to know

The verifier reports a level. The three that matter most:

| Level | Meaning | How you get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `verified` | The chain links and hashes correctly, signatures check under keys you pinned, and the head is proved under the log you pinned. | Pin runner key, log key and log origin. |
| `self-consistent` | The export agrees with itself, but its signatures were checked only against keys the export states. | `--trust-bundle-keys`. |
| `unauthenticated` | No signature was checked. | `--skip-signatures`. |

Other levels (`failed`, `incomplete`, `unsigned`, `unanchored`) cover broken, partial or unanchored exports. The exit code is 0 for `verified`, 5 for a consistent export weaker than verified, and 3 for failure. See [trust levels](/docs/evidence/trust-levels) for the full list.

## What a receipt can prove

Under keys you pinned, `verified` shows that:

- the records were produced by the runner identity you trust and have not been altered or reordered;
- the chain ended as it states, for example `Closed`;
- parts declared not applicable, such as deployment at PR level, are consistent with the chain;
- a particular decision, denial or verifier result is on the record.

## What it cannot prove

`verified` is about provenance and integrity. It does not prove:

- that the code is correct or the tests are thorough;
- that the model reasoned well;
- that a compromised host logged every event it should have;
- statements the export marks `unsourced`, such as credential scopes or network policy when no broker or proxy statement is attested.

Treat evidence as the basis for a good review, not a substitute for one. No page on this site is an audit or certification claim.

## Failure modes

A wrong pinned key produces `FAIL signer_untrusted`, exit 3. A missing key mode is a usage error, because the verifier never defaults to trusting the export.
