## Policy outside the model

Before any privileged action, the request goes to a policy decision made by a component that is not the model. The result is a `DecisionOutcome`: `DECISION_OUTCOME_ALLOW`, `DECISION_OUTCOME_DENY` or `DECISION_OUTCOME_REQUIRE_APPROVAL`. A model cannot talk its way to a different answer, because it is not asked. Denials are recorded in evidence like any other decision.

## Grants and capabilities

A **grant** is explicit, bounded authority: scope, risk class, environment and expiry. A **capability** is a typed ability to perform a kind of action through a governed port. Plugins and executors receive capabilities, not ambient access. Operations in the protocol are annotated with a `RiskClass` (`RISK_CLASS_READ`, `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, `PRIVILEGED`), so the policy knows what an action is before it decides.

Credentials follow the same rule. The design gives providers host-issued credential handles instead of reusable secrets, with an egress proxy applying the token at the boundary. Receipts from those components are chain records.

## Four identities and separation of duties

`PrincipalKind` names who is acting. Four of them carry the separation of duties:

| Identity | Role | Cannot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PRINCIPAL_KIND_AUTHOR_AGENT` | Implements work units. | Approve, review or release its own work. |
| `PRINCIPAL_KIND_REVIEWER_AGENT` | Reviews and runs independent verifiers. | Author the change it reviews. |
| `PRINCIPAL_KIND_RELEASE_PIPELINE` | The only identity that deploys. | Author code. |
| `PRINCIPAL_KIND_HUMAN_APPROVER` | Approves gates. | Approve a change they authored. |

Other principals serve the system: `PRINCIPAL_KIND_RUNNER` writes evidence, and `PRINCIPAL_KIND_BROKER` and `PRINCIPAL_KIND_EGRESS_PROXY` handle credentials and network egress. `PRINCIPAL_KIND_BREAK_GLASS` is described below.

## Protected paths

Some paths, such as the policy that governs the agent, CI definitions and the repository's own ownership rules, are protected. An agent can propose a change to them, but cannot apply it through its normal grant. This stops the obvious escape: editing the rules that bound it.

## Circuit breaker

The breaker is customer-local, so stopping the system never depends on the vendor. `BreakerPosition` has four values: `BREAKER_POSITION_FULL_AUTO`, `BREAKER_POSITION_PR_ONLY`, `BREAKER_POSITION_READ_ONLY` and `BREAKER_POSITION_STOPPED`. Moving to a more restrictive position takes effect over any autonomy level in policy, and in-flight changes pause. Note that `PR_ONLY` is a breaker position, not a ladder level.

## Break-glass

Break-glass is the emergency path, and it is human-only. An agent cannot invoke it, and its use is recorded. It exists so that humans are never locked out by the system's own controls. It is not a way to grant more autonomy.

## Failure modes

- An agent requests an action outside its grant: denied, recorded, change continues or escalates.
- An author tries to approve: rejected, since approver and author must differ.
- An extension is unsigned or its digest does not match: it is not loaded.
